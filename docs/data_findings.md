The calendar audit proves absence, not why those dates are absent. We must not
invent sales values for them. Similarly, a recorded zero could reflect no buyers,
a closure, unavailable stock or a family not stocked; this dataset alone cannot
establish the cause.

## First interview explanation

"I checked the observation grain and unique keys before creating features. The
dataset had a complete store-family panel on observed dates but omitted four
calendar dates. I therefore planned calendar-based lags rather than assuming
seven rows always meant seven days. I also validated the store join and identified
holiday dates with multiple events so joining metadata would not inflate sales."

## Evaluation protocol to implement

Initial protocol: rolling next-day predictions. On each test day, the previous
day's actual sales are available, while that test day's sales are hidden from
features. Model parameters remain fixed during the test. A 28-day test window is
not a forecast of all 28 days made at one origin.

Reserve the final 28 days, 2017-07-19 through 2017-08-15, as the final test.
Use the preceding 28 days, 2017-06-21 through 2017-07-18, for validation.
Neither period is used for initial model fitting; final test labels must not be
used for choosing features, hyperparameters or the reported model.
